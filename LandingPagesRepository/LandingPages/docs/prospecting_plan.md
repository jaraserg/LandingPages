# Customer Acquisition Strategy — LandingPages

This document defines how LandingPages finds, converts, and retains customers for local-business and personal-brand landing pages.

---

## 1. Target Segments

### Segment A — Local Businesses on Google Maps
- **Who:** Restaurants, cafes, barbershops, salons, boutiques, tradespeople, small services with no website or an outdated one.
- **Why now:** Google Maps profiles are becoming mini-directories, but many businesses leave the "Website" field blank or point to an abandoned Facebook page. A modern landing page improves local SEO, trust, and phone orders.
- **Evidence proxy:** Search Maps → inspect profile → if website field is empty, redirects to a generic social page, or the site looks > 4 years old, it's a lead.

### Segment B — Students & Job Seekers
- **Who:** University students, recent graduates, freelancers needing a modern CV/portfolio page.
- **Why now:** Employers and clients increasingly expect an online presence. A polished personal landing page stands out from generic LinkedIn profiles.
- **Evidence proxy:** LinkedIn profiles without a personal site, GitHub repos with no linked portfolio, university career-board posts, resume-review events.

## 2. Outreach Plan

### 2.1 Identification

**Google Maps lead list building**
1. Open Google Maps in an incognito window.
2. Search a category + location: e.g. `restaurantes en Santiago` or `barber cerca de mí`.
3. Open each profile and check:
   - Website field present? Click it.
   - If blank, redirect loop, 404, or old design → add to sheet.
4. Record: Business name, category, address, phone, website status, owner name if visible, Maps URL.
5. Target 20–40 leads per neighborhood/area before outreach.

**Student/job-seeker lead list building**
1. Search LinkedIn for recent graduates in target roles.
2. Check GitHub profiles for README-only portfolios.
3. Check university career portals and Discord/Slack communities.
4. Record: Name, field, LinkedIn/GitHub URLs, current online presence quality.

### 2.3 Conversion Rules
- **First case study:** free for one local business or one student per week, in exchange for a testimonial and permission to show before/after.
- **After first win:** publish the case study on your own landing page and share it in every new outreach message.
- **Referrals:** offer 10–15% discount or one month of small updates for every referral that converts.

## 4. Conversion Funnel

```
Outreach (email/DM/in-person)
      ↓
Interest reply
      ↓
Discovery call / 10-min chat
      ↓
Free case-study offer accepted
      ↓
Intake form / config file sent
      ↓
Draft landing page delivered (24–72h)
      ↓
Feedback round (1 included)
      ↓
Launch + testimonial request
      ↓
Upsell paid tier or monthly care
      ↓
Referral ask
```

### Key Metrics to Track
- Outreach sent / replies received: target > 15%
- Discovery calls booked: target > 40% of replies
- Free case studies accepted: target > 25% of chats
- Paid conversion from free: target > 30%
- Referrals per customer: target > 0.3

## 5. Week-by-Week Launch Plan

### Week 1
- Build your own one-page portfolio showing 2 case studies (can be made-up before real clients).
- Choose 1 neighborhood/category and build a lead list of 30 businesses.
- Send 10 outreach messages per day.

### Week 2
- Follow up with non-responders.
- Deliver first free case study.
- Document the process as a short case-study PDF.

### Week 3
- Use the live case study in all new outreach.
- Start segment B outreach on LinkedIn and university channels.
- Create a simple booking link (Calendly, Google Form) for discovery chats.

### Week 4+
- Convert warm leads to paid.
- Ask for referrals.
- Repeat for new neighborhoods/categories.

## 6. Tools & Assets Needed
- Google Sheets for lead tracking.
- Gmail + WhatsApp for outreach.
- Calendly or Google Forms for scheduling.
- QR-code generator for in-person cards.
- Case-study template (1-page PDF).
- This repo’s templates as production base.

---

## 7. Notes
- Keep first deliveries fast: 24–72h beats “perfect but late.”
- Default to mobile-first layouts; local customers browse on phones.
- Avoid jargon. Sell outcomes: “más pedidos”, “más clientes”, “destacá en tu búsqueda laboral”.
